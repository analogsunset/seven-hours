FELT TEMPERATURES
- apparent temperatures

Bitter Cold: under 8F
Very Cold: 8-15F
Chilly: 16-19F
Comfortable: 20-32F
Warm: 33-45F
Very warm: over 45F

SKY CONDITIONS
- sky cover based on percent of the sky covered by opaque (not transparent) clouds

Bluebird: 5% or less of the sky covered by opaque clouds for the lift day
Sunny: 5% to 12.5% of the sky covered by opaque clouds for the lift day
Mostly Sunny: 12.5% to 37.5% of the sky covered by opaque clouds for the lift day
Partly Sunny: 37.5% to 62.5% of the sky covered by opaque clouds for the lift day
Mostly Cloudy: 62.5% to 87.5% of the sky covered by opaque clouds for the lift day
Cloudy: 87.5% to 100% of the sky covered by opaque clouds for the lift day
Flat Light: low cloud sat on the hill over half the lift day

MEH
Too Warm = Very warm
Too Windy = gusts of 40mph+ for over half of the lift day
Rain = Rain on Snow
Flat Light = Flat Light
Too Cold = Bitter Cold OR Very Cold AND not EPIC
Cloudy and Cold = Mostly Cloudy or Cloudy AND Chilly AND not EPIC

GOOD
Felt temperature = Chilly, Comfortable, or Warm
Sky cover = Bluebird through Cloudy
*If Cloudy or Mostly Cloudy, temperature must be Comfortable or Warm
Flat Light = No Flat Light
Wind = gusts under 40 mph for more than half the lift day
Rain = No Rain on Snow

GREAT
Felt temperature = Comfortable or Warm 
Sky cover = Bluebird through Mostly Sunny
Flat Light = No Flat Light
Wind = gusts under 40 mph for more than half the lift day
Rain = No Rain on Snow
1 week fresh snow = Had 5 inches or more of fresh snow in the last 168 hours if measured by SNOTEL OR above its computed equivalent depth as measured by the model in the last 168 hours.

OR

Felt temperature = Very Cold, Chilly, Comfortable, or Warm 
Sky cover = Bluebird through Cloudy
*If Cloudy or Mostly Cloudy, temperature must be Comfortable or Warm
Flat Light = No Flat Light
Wind = gusts under 40 mph for more than half the lift day
Rain = No Rain on Snow
1 week fresh snow = Had 5 inches or more of fresh snow in the last 168 hours if measured by SNOTEL OR above its computed equivalent depth as measured by the model in the last 168 hours.
24 hour fresh snow = Had 2 inches or more of fresh snow in the last 24 hours if measured by SNOTEL OR above its computed equivalent depth as measured by the model in the last 24 hours.

OR

Felt temperature = Very Cold, Chilly, Comfortable, or Warm 
Sky cover = Bluebird through Cloudy
*If Cloudy or Mostly Cloudy, temperature must be Comfortable or Warm
Flat Light = No Flat Light
Wind = gusts under 40 mph for more than half the lift day
Rain = No Rain on Snow
1 week fresh snow = Had 5 inches or more of fresh snow in the last 168 hours if measured by SNOTEL OR above its computed equivalent depth as measured by the model in the last 168 hours.
72 hour fresh snow = Had 5 inches or more of fresh snow in the last 72 hours if measured by SNOTEL OR above its computed equivalent depth as measured by the model in the last 72 hours.

EPIC
Felt temperature = Very Cold, Chilly, Comfortable, or Warm 
Sky cover = Bluebird through Cloudy
*If Very Cold, must be Partly Sunny, Mostly Sunny, Sunny, or Bluebird
Flat Light = No Flat Light
Wind = gusts under 40 mph for more than half the lift day
Rain = No Rain on Snow
1 week fresh snow = Had 5 inches or more of fresh snow in the last 168 hours if measured by SNOTEL OR above its computed equivalent depth as measured by the model in the last 168 hours.
24 hour fresh snow = Had 4 inches or more of fresh snow in the last 24 hours if measured by SNOTEL OR above its computed equivalent depth as measured by the model in the last 24 hours.




ADDENDUM (NOT RULES, JUST NOTES)
Based on the data you have access to in the database, propose a more accurate way to measure Wind. For example, a single peak gust > 40 mph from 9-10am is very different from sustained winds.

NOTES ON WHAT THE DATA CAN ACTUALLY SUPPORT
- WIND is measured on GUSTS, not sustained speed. ERA5's 10m wind is a ~25 km
  grid-cell mean: across 4.4 million lift hours it averages 5.3 mph and reaches
  40.0 mph exactly once, so a sustained-40 rule would never fire in 27 winters.
  The gust field (mean 20.3 mph, max 136) is the one carrying mountain wind.
  The "over half the lift day" duration is applied to it, and fires on 4.9% of
  days against 9.5% for the old any-single-hour rule.
- The MODEL side of every snow test is a computed equivalent DEPTH, not a
  percentile: the inch figure converted into that resort's own modelled scale.
  A per-resort percentile ranks each mountain against itself and so hands every
  one of them the same quota of big-snow days however little it snows. Under it
  Mt. Lemmon -- about ten inches of snow in a whole season -- had a 0.17 inch
  "4-inch" threshold and scored more Epic days than Alta.
- SKY COVER uses the greater of the low and mid cloud layers, high cirrus
  excluded, quantised to 2 points before anything is tested so the band label
  and the printed percentage can never disagree.
